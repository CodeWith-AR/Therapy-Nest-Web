Academic Whitepaper & Methodology

Clinical Science & Adaptive Engine Architecture

Bridging the neuroplastic rehabilitation dosage gap through edge-computed Item Response Theory and private speech recognition.

Paper Abstract (JMIR / Frontiers in Digital Health)

Title: Therapy Nest: An Open-Source, IRT-Adaptive Mobile Platform for Cognitive and Speech Rehabilitation in Resource-Constrained Settings — Design, Architecture, and Pilot Evaluation

Background: Over 15 million individuals suffer strokes worldwide annually, with 30–40% acquiring aphasia. While neuroplastic recovery requires 4+ hours of weekly intensive practice, standard healthcare systems average less than 45 minutes of weekly in-clinic therapy. Commercial digital therapeutics impose recurring subscription costs ($30/month) that exclude underserved populations.

Methods: We developed Therapy Nest, a free, offline-first digital therapy system combining an on-device Vosk acoustic speech recognizer with a 2-Parameter Logistic (2PL) Item Response Theory adaptive engine. The algorithm maintains patient success rates within the optimal flow channel (70–80%).

1. The Rehabilitation Dosage Crisis

Neuroscience demonstrates that neuroplastic restructuring following stroke or traumatic brain injury is dosage-dependent. Patients who practice self-directed therapy daily show significantly superior functional landmark recovery compared to those limited to weekly clinic visits alone.

Therapy Nest removes the economic barrier by creating a permanently free, open-source alternative to proprietary commercial apps.

2. Item Response Theory (2PL IRT) Formulation

Instead of coarse, static difficulty levels, Therapy Nest models each patient's latent cognitive and speech ability as a continuous parameter θ ∈ [-3.0, +3.0]. The probability of a correct response on item i is calculated using the 2-Parameter Logistic model:

Pi(θ) = 1 / (1 + e-1.702 · ai (θ - bi))

Where θ = Patient latent ability · bi = Item difficulty parameter · ai = Item discrimination

Real-Time Elo Calibration Engine

After each exercise attempt, the patient's domain ability score is updated instantaneously:

θnew = θold + K · (Outcome - Expected)
Where K = 32.0 (scaled by hint penalty factor) and Expected = Pi(θ)

3. Clinical Cueing Hierarchy & Partial Scoring

To prevent frustration and promote errorless learning, Therapy Nest implements a standardized 4-level cueing hierarchy:

Level Assistance Type Clinical Description Score Credit
Level 0 Independent Stimulus presented with no assistance 1.0 (100%)
Level 1 Semantic Cue Functional clue ("It's something you drink from") 0.7 (70%)
Level 2 Phonemic Cue First sound / letter prompt ("Starts with /k/") 0.7 (70%)
Level 3 Visual Choice Multiple-choice reduction to 2 options 0.3 (30%)
Level 4 Full Model Correct answer spoken & revealed for repetition 0.0 (0%)
